Apple, Samsung and Google's recycling and circular economy programmes in the face of the right to repair

← Retour à l'accueil

The global smartphone industry is gradually entering a new phase: producing more is no longer sufficient; extending device lifespan, recovering materials, and facilitating repairs are also necessary. This shift is driven by environmental policies, the rise of electronic recycling, and the development of the “right to repair,” which aims to give consumers more options to repair their devices rather than replace them. Apple, Samsung, and Google, three major players in the mobile market, have each developed programmes focused on recycling, repair, and reuse. However, their strategies exhibit significant differences.
