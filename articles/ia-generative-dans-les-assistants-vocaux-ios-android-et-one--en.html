Generative AI in Voice Assistants: iOS, Android, and One UI Face to Face in 2024

← Retour à l'accueil

In 2024, mobile giants are heavily investing in generative artificial intelligence to make their voice assistants smarter, more contextual, and proactive, thereby transforming everyday user interaction.

Siri and Apple’s Generative AI

Apple has integrated a large language model, called Apple Neural Engine 2, into Siri, enabling more natural responses, text-to-code synthesis, and email summarization, while maintaining its privacy-focused approach through on-device processing on recent hardware.

Google Assistant and Android’s Generative AI

Google leverages its Gemini model, hosted in the cloud, to power Google Assistant. In 2024, Gemini provides multimodal content generation, contextual instant translation, and the ability to execute complex actions via natural-language commands, taking advantage of the massive Google Cloud infrastructure.

Bixby in One UI: Samsung’s Strategy

Samsung has chosen to integrate a proprietary model called Samsung AI Lab, paired with the Exynos AI chip, to equip Bixby with text-generation and home-automation capabilities, while aiming to differentiate its assistant through tighter integration with Galaxy devices and QLED displays.

Challenges, Performance, and the Future of Assistants in 2024

The three players compete on accuracy, latency, and data protection. Apple bets on on-device processing, Google on cloud power, and Samsung on the hardware ecosystem. Ongoing challenges include energy consumption, AI-generated misinformation, and the need for clear regulation.

Conclusion

In summary, the race for generative AI is reshaping the voice-assistant landscape: each platform leverages its strengths—privacy, cloud power, or hardware integration—to deliver richer experiences, while still needing to overcome technical and ethical hurdles before dominating the market.
